## 1. What Project Sprint Means by "Project"

In these documents, the word "project" is used on assumptions that differ somewhat from the conventional ones.

A project is commonly understood as an activity in which a plan is executed toward a goal or a right answer that was set in advance. Real projects, however, exist in uncertain situations, and their environment and requirements keep changing. The assumptions and plans set at the outset therefore often become difficult to execute as they are.

In Project Sprint, a project is an activity in which the members decide by their own will how to bring their activity to convergence amid a constantly changing situation, and then find the next direction based on the results.

A project is not something given in advance; it is something deliberately carved out. And its goal is not fixed; it is updated by the will of the team, which takes shape through action and dialogue.

Please read these documents with a project understood not as something that moves toward a fixed right answer, but as an activity that discovers value and direction in response to the situation.


## 2. What Project Sprint Is Trying to Change

The world is in a state of uncertainty, and so is each project we face. Environments and requirements keep changing, and the future cannot be fully predicted.

The conventional view of projects has tried to cope with this uncertainty by making "more accurate plans." Defining the right answer in advance and executing it efficiently was regarded as project success.<br>
On this premise, the plan always precedes reality, and action is positioned as its execution. In reality, however, the premises of a plan collapse easily, and the project is forced to revise it each time. Eventually, maintaining the plan becomes an end in itself, and the project loses any real sense of moving forward.

Perhaps the problem lies not in the accuracy of the plan, but in how we see the project itself.<br>
Project Sprint turns this view of projects around.

In Project Sprint, a project is not an activity that executes a right answer set in advance. It is an activity that updates understanding through action and discovers value amid uncertainty.
In other words, a project is exploration. In exploration, the plan is only a starting point, and progress comes from action. Action, and the output that results from it, generate new understanding and make the next decision possible.

What becomes important here is the presence of the individuals involved in the project. Each person has different experience, knowledge, and interests, and any understanding of the world is always personal and partial. There is a limit to the reality a single person can grasp. In uncertain situations, this limit becomes the limit of the project itself. For a project to move in a better direction, it therefore needs to take in diverse perspectives rather than a single one.

Project Sprint assumes that each member brings their own differing perceptions and sensibilities into the project through action and output, and that these are then integrated through dialogue. When diverse individuals take part in exploration from their own perspectives, the project does not close itself within a single understanding, and moves toward the realization of richer value.

Members, then, are not merely people who carry out a role. Each is an agent who acts on their own interests, unease, and insights, and brings the results into the project. Project Sprint presupposes this kind of self-directed action by each individual. Only when members exercise their abilities and take part in exploration of their own accord does exploration by the project as a whole come into being.

On these assumptions, Project Sprint reconstructs the way a project is run. It is a shift from a project that realizes a value set in advance to a project that keeps exploring value. The purpose of a project is not to answer the question posed at the outset. It is to keep updating the question itself through action and output, while moving toward the realization of whatever value feels better at that point in time. What matters in a project is therefore not conformity to the plan, but continually updating the question and the purpose as the situation changes.

Project Sprint is not a method for running projects well. It is a framework that changes how we see projects, and that deals with the very process by which people and teams bring value into being.


## 3. The Value Project Sprint Provides

To move a project forward in uncertain situations, Project Sprint designs a rhythm of two activities: exploration and convergence.<br>
Exploration is the activity of understanding the situation through action and widening the possibilities.<br>
Convergence is the activity of setting the direction of the next action based on those results.<br>
Through this succession of exploration and convergence, a project moves forward while gradually sharpening its focus.

The value Project Sprint provides is to realize, through this structure of successive exploration and convergence, a state in which the project keeps moving forward and an environment in which autonomous collaboration can take place. By understanding and practicing Project Sprint, the way a project proceeds changes in itself.

First, the project comes to move forward through output rather than through plans.<br>
The artifacts and insights produced as the result of individual action become the primary information with which the team understands the world, and the basis for the next decision. Rather than tracing a plan set in advance, the project proceeds from the output of exploration, updating its understanding through periodic convergence.
Here, the information gained through action gives direction to the next decision. Instead of fixing the right answer in advance, the direction ahead becomes gradually clear on the basis of actual action and its results.

The way people engage with the project also changes.<br>
Project Sprint presupposes that diverse members take part in exploration autonomously, each from their own perspective. Each person has different experience and interests and grasps the world in a different way. In uncertain situations, this very diversity is the source that widens the project's understanding.
When multiple perspectives are brought to bear on a reality that no single perspective can fully grasp, the project moves in a direction the team can feel convinced is better. In today's projects, diversity is not an option but a given, and whether it can be put to use decides the outcome.
Project Sprint enables each member to act from their own perspective and bring the results into the project. This allows the project to move toward the realization of value as a whole team, without depending on any particular individual or role.

The way decisions are made changes as well.<br>
In Project Sprint, decisions are made on the basis of conviction (*nattoku*) rather than correctness. Rather than seeking the one right answer, it values a state in which the team is able to take on the next action.
Decision-making based on conviction draws out self-directed action from members. When people are convinced by their own judgment, they can take on the action as their own. As a result, a team emerges that moves autonomously rather than being moved by control.

Through these changes, Project Sprint transforms the project from "something that executes a plan" into "something that keeps discovering value through exploration."


## 4. How to Experience, Understand, and Practice Project Sprint

Understanding Project Sprint does not come from reading alone. Its value is recognized only through action and experience.<br>
Project Sprint therefore recommends proceeding, wherever possible, in the following order.

- Workshop: experience the rhythm of exploration and convergence
- CODE: put the experience into words and understand it as a structure
- Practical Guide: apply it to real projects

In the Workshop, you actually experience the cycle of exploration and convergence in a project.<br>
This experience is not necessarily understood in clear words, but it remains as a felt sense gained through action and results.

CODE exists to give meaning to that experience.<br>
By putting into words what was happening in the Workshop and reframing it as the assumptions and structure of Project Sprint, you become able to reinterpret the experience.

The Practical Guide exists to connect that understanding to practice.<br>
It shows concretely how to act and how to proceed in a real project.

This order is based not merely on learning efficiency, but on the very assumptions of Project Sprint. People cannot act only after fully understanding the meaning in advance. Understanding comes into being only by experiencing action and its results, and interpreting them afterwards.<br>
First comes experience, and understanding follows. Project Sprint is designed in this order.<br>
Through this flow, Project Sprint is understood not as knowledge, but as a framework that actually works.


## 5. The Design Philosophy and Structure of CODE

Project Sprint is not something to adopt as a set of procedures as it is. It takes effect only when it is accepted with an understanding of the thinking behind it.<br>
Project Sprint can be applied generally, but its effect depends on how far the underlying assumptions and thinking are shared.<br>
If only the structure is introduced without standing on these assumptions, a gap can arise between it and the way the project is actually run, and its effect may not be fully realized.

CODE puts the thinking and structure of Project Sprint into words on the basis of the above.<br>
It is not, however, a theory to be understood first and applied afterwards. Taking the experience of the Workshop as its premise, the role of this document is to give meaning to that experience, to make it possible to understand what was happening as a structure, and at the same time to present the underlying assumptions and thinking and provide criteria for judgment in practice.

CODE therefore presents not only the theory and structure of Project Sprint, but also the thinking that underlies them.

CODE is divided into three Steps, according to the stage of understanding and the difficulty of the practice that follows.

[Step0](./step0) deals with the individual acting on their own initiative and updating their understanding of the world through output.<br>
[Step1](./step1) deals with multiple individuals forming a shared understanding through output and dialogue, and carrying exploration forward as a team.<br>
Step2 deals with how, as a result of that exploration, the team generates value and brings change to society.<br>
(Step2 will be written in v5.1 or later and is not included as of v5.0.)

## 6. What We Expect of Readers After Reading CODE

When you finish reading these documents, you will not be in a state of having fully understood Project Sprint. Nor is the aim for you to be able to apply what is written here exactly as it stands.

What these documents give you is a perspective for grasping what is happening in a project.<br>
You become able to put into words, and understand, the structure behind what you experienced in the Workshop and the events of your actual projects.

These documents also provide criteria for judgment in practice. What should be prioritized in a project? How should decisions be made? How should one relate to other members? They do not give a single right answer to these questions, but they give you an axis for thinking about them on the basis of the assumptions of Project Sprint.

As a result, you become able to treat Project Sprint not as a mere technique, but as a framework you can interpret, choose from, and apply according to the situation. Project Sprint is not something you use after understanding it. It is something whose understanding deepens as you use it. These documents provide the footing on which that understanding can take shape.
