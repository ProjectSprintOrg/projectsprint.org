# Key Terms

> [!NOTE]
> **Position of this chapter:**
>
> This chapter gives the definitions of the main terms used in Project Sprint.<br>
> Some of these terms are used with nuances that differ from their everyday meaning. They are presented here as the reference point for reading these documents with a consistent understanding.
>
> **Authors' intent:**
>
> Define the meaning of the key concepts used in Project Sprint, and prevent the interpretation of terms from drifting.
>
> **Value for the reader:**
>
> Understand precisely the concepts that matter when reading the Framework, and grasp the words used in these documents with a shared understanding.

- **Team**: A group of members who take part in a meeting within a project with a common purpose, and who produce output toward that purpose.
- **Project team**: A group of members who hold a shared understanding of the project's purpose and collaborate autonomously toward achieving it. A kind of team; exceptionally, it may have no meeting in which all members take part, or may include members in an advisory position who do not produce output.
- **Project**: An activity that a project team undertakes of its own accord in order to bring some change or value to the society outside the project.
- **Activity**: A physical action, taken by a team or a member with will and purpose, aimed at changing one state into another. An activity always includes output.
- **Output**: The act by which a project member takes action and produces an artifact.
- **Outcome**: A change that the team has brought about inside or outside the project as the result of output; a result toward the realization of value.
- **Value**: What is recognized as meaning or benefit by external stakeholders, through the outcomes of the project.
- **Artifact**: Something with a concrete form, produced by a team member and shared with the team, that other members can interpret and respond to.

> [!TIP]
> **A note on two terms in this translation**
>
> - **Conviction** translates the Japanese <span lang="ja">納得</span> (*nattoku*): a state in which a person, on some grounds, is able to feel that something is good, and can therefore take on an action as their own. It is not mere agreement or compliance.
> - **Shared understanding** translates <span lang="ja">共通了解</span> (*kyōtsū ryōkai*). As Step1 explains, it does not mean that members hold identical understanding. It is a state in which members, while holding different understandings, grasp one another's thinking and situation roughly enough to act together.
